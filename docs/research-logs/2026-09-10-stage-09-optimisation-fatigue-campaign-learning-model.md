## 4. Seven tracked records

Reuse Stage 3's stable identities, revisions, real/synthetic context, provenance, uncertainty, scoped use decisions and unresolved-item ownership. Reuse Stage 8's observation/analysis contracts. The records can be sections and tables in a single campaign file; seven logical responsibilities do not require seven files, services or agents.

### L01. Parent concept

**Record.** Concept ID/revision, parent territory/message/brief, approved business and audience baseline, creative mechanism, supported claim/proof and CTA dependencies, selected fixed elements, open treatment choices, owner and exact selection evidence. Reference an existing accepted concept rather than generating a competing copy with new parents.

**Decision and evidence.** Distinguish semantic descent from source evidence. A new variant may descend from a successful concept, but does not inherit measured success, rights, current claim adequacy or publication authority. The concept's original hypothesis and reasons for selection remain accessible alongside subsequent evidence.

**Failure and repair.** An absent parent, circular revision ancestry or a new commercial proposition disguised as a minor refresh blocks the dependent lineage/approval claim. Correct the link or create a genuinely new, reviewed concept; preserve the old record. Do not erase a valid concept because one execution had a technical defect.

### L02. Variants

**Record.** Variant ID/revision, exact concept/master parents, content or actual asset/component references, requested/delivered fidelity, proof/qualification dependencies, locale/format, assembly rules, specialist provenance and review scope. Distinguish a proposed variant, a produced asset and a use episode in a particular delivery context.

**Decision and evidence.** A use episode identifies the exact variant and media/destination/configuration revisions, start/end context and actual execution evidence. One asset can have several episodes; one episode can use a controlled assembled component set. External IDs are mappings, not proof that creative meaning changed. Same bytes can receive different external IDs, and a stable ID can be attached to changing content.

**Failure and repair.** A renamed asset is not a refresh, a proposed filename is not an actual output, and a platform preview is not every served combination. Repair the affected mapping, obtain the missing actual artefact or constrain the transformation. Preserve valid siblings and source assets; no universal renderer or platform adapter is introduced.

### L03. Changed elements

**Record.** Change ID, reason and evidence, prior and proposed revisions, exact differences, fixed elements, affected claim/proof/brand/offer/audience/CTA dependencies, context changes, responsible owner, approval consequences, test question and required rechecks. Separate a content revision from an execution-configuration change and a source-data correction.

**Decision and evidence.** A refresh can change a hook, proof presentation, pace, layout or placement treatment while preserving the selected concept. Record every other consequential difference, including automatic enhancement, targeting expansion, goal and destination changes. Use the smallest representation capable of checking the changed meaning before expensive production. A complete creative-system comparison is allowed when explicitly identified as such.

**Failure and repair.** Do not call a price change a copy edit or a simultaneous audience/creative change a hook-only test. Return commercial changes upstream. Correct the change description or comparison before interpreting results. Previous approvals apply only to their recorded scope and revision; unchanged meaning does not automatically renew expired rights or constraints.

### L04. Audience/placement context

**Record.** Exact approved segment, audience hypothesis and targeting representation; placement/provider product and profile; geography/locale; inventory/device/modality; date/window and timezone; offer/destination/goal versions; allocation and optimisation policy; exclusions; and observed versus planned reach/frequency, counted unit, overlap and exposure distribution where available.

**Decision and evidence.** State whether exposure describes people, devices, accounts, households, impressions or modelled aggregates. Separate cumulative exposure to a concept family from exposure to one external asset ID when evidence permits; otherwise preserve unknown overlap. Link frequency and results to comparable populations and windows. Do not manufacture person-level histories or infer sensitive audience traits to complete the record.

**Failure and repair.** A changed audience mix, delivery algorithm, destination or outcome horizon can invalidate the intended comparison without proving fatigue. Obtain compatible strata/context or limit the conclusion. An exposure setting is not observed exposure, and cross-provider averages cannot establish a deduplicated person-level dose. Repair the context mapping rather than rewriting the creative to fit a misleading aggregate.

### L05. Results

**Record.** Stage 8 observation and analysis references, exact test/arm/variant/use episode, metric and source versions, platform and verified downstream outcomes separately, denominators, cohort/time/maturity, uncertainty, attribution/causal scope, validity diagnostics, guardrail findings and the predeclared decision rule. Retain unavailable, immature, invalid, modelled and actual zero distinctly.

**Decision and evidence.** A result is about a defined comparison or observation, not an intrinsic score embedded forever in an asset. Preserve raw provider ratings as provider observations with their date and comparison basis. A favourable primary result and an acceptable downstream business outcome are different conclusions. A diagnosis of invalid tracking does not make the creative a demonstrated loser.

**Failure and repair.** Missing event meaning, incompatible periods, unresolved observation loss, duplicate counts or an unqualified proxy blocks the affected conclusion. Route to the measurement/execution owner before creative replacement. Restatements create a new evidence version and impact review; do not silently overwrite the report that informed a historical decision. [Stage8]

### L06. Learning

**Record.** Learning ID/revision, question, source observations, supported inference and alternative explanations, evidence class/strength, uncertainty, exact applicability context, practical implication, selected/rejected action, owner and decision, contrary evidence, reuse conditions and review trigger. Separate a measured result from a possible explanation and a generalisation beyond the measured population.

**Decision and evidence.** Learning can state that a test was invalid, the offer could not be fulfilled, a comparison remained inconclusive or a promised event was not measurable. Such findings can be useful without becoming creative-effectiveness claims. Retain rejected routes and why they were rejected so a later agent does not regenerate an unsupported claim as a fresh idea.

**Failure and repair.** A universal instruction such as “always lead with price” cannot follow from one context-specific comparison. Narrow the lesson or seek independent evidence. Do not reclassify an untested alternative as unsuccessful, a synthetic review as consumer research or a repeated report as independent corroboration. New contradictory evidence reopens the affected inference while preserving the earlier observation and decision context.

### L07. Lifecycle state

**Record.** Subject ID/revision, use/assessment scope, state facet, previous and new values, effective time and record time, reason, evidence and decision references, issuer/authority, conditions, expiry/review trigger and downstream impact. Events describe changes; a current view is derived from the latest applicable accepted events, with unresolved conflicts visible.

**Decision and evidence.** Separate delivery, performance assessment, evidence validity, fatigue finding and succession. The named labels active, winner, loser, fatigued, invalid and superseded remain available with exact meanings in section 5. Creative selection is not activation; a requested pause is not a completed pause; a newly produced replacement is not automatically adopted.

**Failure and repair.** Missing scope, incompatible states for the same facet and overlapping period, unverified execution or a supersession link to an absent replacement blocks the affected state claim. Keep the disputed event and route resolution to its owner; do not infer authority from the newest timestamp. These are advertising-domain records, not Pactwright Contracts, project lifecycle topology or a new orchestration runtime.

## 5. Lifecycle semantics and transitions

### Independent facets

| Facet | Values and scope | Evidence requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery | proposed, active, paused or ended for the use episode and observed interval | Actual authorised executor evidence for active/paused/ended; proposals and requests stay separate |
| Performance assessment | untested, inconclusive, winner, loser or withdrawn for a named comparison/criterion/context | Valid, sufficiently mature evidence under Stage 8 and the recorded decision rule; a desk-review preference is labelled preferred-for-review, not a market winner |
| Evidence validity | unreviewed, valid-for-scope, limited or invalid for a specific observation/analysis | Reasoned validity findings and affected scope; invalid evidence cannot support a new effectiveness conclusion |
| Fatigue finding | not-assessed, suspected, fatigued or not-supported for a defined exposure/outcome context | Section 6's evidence and alternatives; record operational diagnosis versus demonstrated causal mechanism |
| Succession | current or superseded for a defined use/selection scope | Accepted replacement/adoption or explicit successor link and owner decision; planned work is not an adopted successor |

Use the exact existing approval and knowledge records from earlier stages; the facet values do not replace them. The content revision can remain current for one placement while its use is superseded elsewhere. Winner and loser are mutually exclusive only for the same assessment criterion, comparison and period. Different questions can legitimately produce different results.

### Required named labels

| Label | Exact meaning | Prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|
| active | The specified use episode is actually enabled/delivering as described by current executor evidence, within its stated limits | A media plan or approval alone proves serving, audience reach or effectiveness |
| winner | The recorded valid comparison meets its named favourable criterion in its context and horizon | Universal superiority, automatic profitability, permanent success or spend authority |
| loser | The recorded valid comparison meets its named unfavourable criterion | No statistical significance, no data, an untested alternative or a broken tracker proves failure |
| fatigued | Evidence supports an operational repetition-related deterioration diagnosis for the specified context under section 6 | Age, high average frequency, a low platform rating or one aggregate drop proves causal wearout |
| invalid | A named observation/analysis cannot support its intended conclusion because a recorded validity requirement failed | Every historical result, the concept and all siblings must be deleted or labelled losers |
| superseded | A named revision/use/selection has an accepted successor for the stated scope; the old record remains historical | The old item was ineffective, is deleted, or cannot remain in an unrelated valid use |

“Active” does not guarantee that an impression was delivered in every minute of the interval. Preserve whether the return establishes enabled configuration, observed serving or both. A setting, a requested action and an observed result remain different evidence. Unknown current execution cannot be reported as confirmed paused simply because pausing was recommended.

### Transition checks

| ID | Proposed transition | Required checks and preservation |
|---|---|---|
| E01 | proposed → active | Exact actual variant/context, applicable approvals and scoped executor return; no activation from an empty manifest or request |
| E02 | active → paused/ended | Authorised action and returned evidence with effective time; retain requested/failed attempts and observations from any uncontained interval |
| E03 | untested/inconclusive → winner/loser | Valid comparison, mature relevant outcomes, declared criterion and scoped assessment; primary and downstream conclusions stay distinct |
| E04 | unreviewed/valid-for-scope/limited → invalid | Identify failed rule, data/analysis window and dependent conclusions; preserve independent earlier valid evidence and accepted creative |
| E05 | not-assessed/suspected → fatigued/not-supported | Inspect exposure, comparable results, other causes and declared evidence threshold; absence of support is not proof that fatigue is impossible |
| E06 | current → superseded | Existing accepted successor, substitution scope, adoption decision and affected usage mapping; do not alter unrelated placements or delete history |
| E07 | paused/fatigued/superseded work → proposed new use | Recheck current truth, rights, context, residual exposure and authority; create a linked new episode/revision as appropriate, not a fabricated reset of audience memory |

A historical winner can remain in the record when a later period is fatigued or invalid. A new audit that invalidates the original winning evidence must record the dependent assessment as withdrawn for current use, while retaining its historical winning decision and its reason. Withdrawal of a conclusion is not deletion of its record. A discovery of unsupported claims separately blocks the affected publication path even when performance evidence remains statistically valid.

Every transition records both when it took effect and when it became known. Corrections are new attributed records, not silent rewrites. Where legitimate privacy/retention obligations prohibit keeping source data, follow the owning decision and preserve only permitted references/limitations; “preserve learning” never authorises indefinite private-data retention. Conflicting state reports are resolved by authority and evidence, not last-write-wins convenience.

## 6. Fatigue diagnosis and evidence threshold

F01 defines a diagnostic responsibility, not an always-on detector. Creative fatigue is considered when repeated exposure to a defined expression/concept in a specified context may be associated with a decline in the relevant audience response. Audience saturation, auction cost, measurement failure, seasonality, changed delivery mix, weak initial creative and upstream offer problems are not interchangeable with fatigue.

| Step | Question and minimum evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 signal | Which outcome changed, relative to which baseline, cohort and window? | Record the trigger and uncertainty; no label from operator boredom or calendar age |
| F2 evidence health | Are event definitions, source data, reporting windows and outcome maturity comparable and sufficiently valid? | Investigate missing/invalid data first; preserve the creative and label the affected inference |
| F3 exposure/context | What repeated exposure is evidenced, to which unit, expression/concept, audience and placement? What changed in the mix or delivery settings? | Retain dose/overlap limitations; no person-level inference from a device average or renamed asset |
| F4 alternatives | Can auction costs, audience expansion, suppression, destination, offer, competing activity or production defects explain the signal? | Record supporting/contrary evidence per plausible cause and route the smallest discriminating check |
| F5 diagnosis strength | Is the evidence sufficient for a bounded operational diagnosis, or does the proposed statement require causal identification? | Use suspected, fatigued with an explicit operational limit, or not-supported; retain unresolved issues separately and do not promote correlation to mechanism |
| F6 response and recheck | What authorised minimal change or new comparison addresses the supported cause, and what would disconfirm it? | Preserve fixed work, use the full Stage 8 test contract when testing, and review after appropriate observation maturation |

Before calling an episode fatigued, the diagnosis record must identify the outcome and meaningful deterioration criterion, valid baseline/comparison and maturity, material exposure context, relevant alternative causes investigated, unresolved limitations, evidence supporting repetition as a working explanation, owner and permitted decision. The threshold is declared for the campaign question; no universal frequency, percentage fall or time-in-market is introduced. A hypothesis can justify a cheap investigation without justifying a stronger label or an expensive full replacement.

An operational diagnosis is a reasoned basis for a scoped action under uncertainty. It must explicitly state when repetition has not been causally isolated. A causal wearout assertion requires an appropriate comparison and identification of repetition effects under Stage 8, including actual assignment/selection and interference limits. Comparing exposed and unexposed audiences observationally, or older and newer ads, does not automatically isolate that mechanism.

A fresh creative outperforming an old one can reflect intrinsic creative quality, changed delivery or audience selection as well as repetition. Even a clean randomised old-versus-new comparison establishes that contrast, not every explanation for it. Design the next comparison to answer the unresolved cause only when the decision needs that additional evidence. Never force a costly causal study for a small reversible, already authorised creative investigation, but preserve the limited inference.

Use relevant time horizons. Immediate dislike, later recall, qualified response and retained business value can differ. Report them against the accepted objective, without averaging a hard customer-harm finding away or asserting that future memory will compensate. A cooling-off period is a hypothesis to test, not a guaranteed reset. Changing an asset ID or format is not evidence that prior exposure disappeared.

The absence of a fatigue finding may mean not assessed, insufficient information or evidence not supporting this particular hypothesis. It does not prove infinite creative life. Conversely, high frequency is not by itself a defect when the declared context and objective support it. The decision remains constrained by approved targeting, truth, customer experience, economics and actual authority.

## 7. Optimisation, correction and preservation

O01 starts with the business/campaign objective and the seven responsible layers in the bootstrap. Measurement validity is checked across those layers before interpreting performance. Scope the decision: investigation, local repair, creative refresh, new experiment, reallocation, scale or retirement. Each needs different evidence and authority; none follows automatically from a single dashboard ratio.

### Layer diagnosis

| Layer | Evidence question | Smallest responsible response and owner |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery | Was the intended asset/configuration served, to the intended extent and period, and are observations trustworthy? | Authorised ad operations fixes assignment, status, upload or delivery; analytics resolves corrupted observations before creative conclusions |
| Audience | Is the hypothesis weak, the targeting representation different, or qualification/suppression broken? | Advertising revises only an authorised hypothesis; executor repairs mapping; commercial segment changes return to Business Building |
| Creative | Does the actual expression convey the supported message in context, and is repetition a supported contributor? | Advertising/specialist repairs the implicated hook, sequence, proof treatment or master; preserve unaffected claims and concepts |
| Placement | Is inventory, attention context, assembly or allocation incompatible with the expression or comparison? | Correct the placement treatment or propose an authorised media change; preserve the parent message where valid |
| Destination | Does the journey honour price, claim, proof, CTA and event semantics? | UIUX/Software/destination owner repairs the exact continuity or implementation defect; no covert offer change |
| Policy/claims | Is a required constraint, substantiation, right or approval missing, expired or contradicted? | Block affected use and request appropriate evidence/Legal/rights review; persuasion cannot repair absent truth |
| Upstream business | Do qualified response, retention, economics or capacity expose a business constraint? | Business Building/project owner decides the commercial correction; Advertising preserves the evidence without optimising around the constraint |

R02 supplies examples of provider diagnostics that can aid investigation, not final causal answers. R03 illustrates a particularly important separation: a bidding-data remedy and an accurate historical report are different responsibilities. The project model therefore records interventions and evidence corrections separately; neither implies the other succeeded. [R02] [R03]

### Bounded correction contract

| Decision element | Minimum content |
|---|---|
| Cause and scope | Observed symptom, affected variant/use/window, evidence, confidence, alternative causes and why this layer is responsible |
| Proposed change | Exact prior/new content or configuration, changed dimensions, expected mechanism and unresolved dependencies |
| Preservation set | Approved offer/price/segment, concept/master, claims/proof, brand, unaffected assets, context and historical evidence that must remain unchanged |
| Ownership and authority | Responsible producer, acceptance reviewer, actual resource/data/publication/spend authority and any decision needing another owner |
| Representation and cost | Cheapest useful form for the next question, required final output if committed, cost/liability constraints and feasibility |
| Verification and comparison | Relevant claim, semantic, technical, context and measurement checks; complete Stage 8 contract for a new test; actual returned output evidence |
| Completion and learning | Actual action/output identity, findings, approved adoption or rejection, impact on states, residual uncertainty and review trigger |

Inspect the returned correction, not only its request. A caption repositioning needs an actual revised output and contextual review at production scope; it does not require regeneration of unrelated variants. A changed objective or offer may require broader re-evaluation, because the dependency impact is broader, not because all repairs must be small in file count.

Refresh planning moves through selected cause, cheap candidate representation, claim/brand/meaning check, explicit production request, actual asset evaluation and authorised use. A new candidate remains proposed until its production and adoption gates pass. Do not count an outline, script or renamed old asset as a finished refresh. Use Stage 5 for craft/fidelity and Stage 7 for continuity rather than duplicating their whole process.

Scaling changes delivery exposure, marginal economics or capacity and can invalidate transport of a previous result. Specify what is being scaled, which context changes and what evidence/guardrails are required. Only the actual budget owner and executor can release funding or change account settings. Retirement preserves the reason and historical evidence; a recommendation does not prove delivery stopped or liabilities ended.

Safety and truth failures can require immediate authorised containment without waiting for an efficacy threshold. Record the request, actual return and any interval in which the problem remained live. Do not assume rollback is safe when terms or rights have changed. Independent accepted evidence remains available unless the new finding actually undermines it.

## 8. Learning retention, reuse and alternatives

A learning entry retains observation, inference, decision and outcome as distinct fields. At reuse, check the original question, product/offer version, audience/context, exposure, metric/horizon, method validity, business constraints and applicable current rights/policy decisions. Reuse can mean retaining a useful hypothesis, not assuming its result transfers. Research evidence, production knowledge and market effectiveness have different evidential roles.

| Learning situation | Correct retention and reuse |
|---|---|
| Supported bounded result | Preserve comparison, scope, uncertainty and downstream limits; propose reuse only after a context-fit check |
| Inconclusive comparison | Preserve what the test could and could not resolve; no loser label or cherry-picked metric |
| Invalid measurement period | Preserve incident, affected evidence and corrective action; operational learning survives without an effectiveness conclusion |
| Rejected unsupported concept | Preserve the absent proof/claim dependency and reason so the same unsafe proposition is not regenerated |
| Fatigue finding | Preserve exposure/context, alternatives, diagnosis strength and review trigger; do not ban the concept everywhere |
| Contradictory later evidence | Retain both observations, update the inference with an attributed revision and recheck dependent decisions |
| Privacy/rights restriction | Retain only permitted metadata/references and the resulting limitation; never publish private data or imply inaccessible proof was verified |

A concise learning digest must link to the source records and include material negative findings. It cannot replace the durable evidence with a confident slogan. Avoid manually maintaining a universal graph; exact record references and tool-generated checks are sufficient. No automated graph service, fatigue database or platform account store is introduced by this stage.

| Alternative compared | Decision and rationale |
|---|---|
| Single exclusive campaign status versus scoped facets | SELECT facets: delivery, assessment, validity, fatigue and succession can change independently |
| Replace creative on a fixed calendar versus diagnose context | SELECT diagnosis-led response; calendar can schedule review, not establish fatigue |
| Treat provider Best/Low as winner/loser versus preserve raw ratings | SELECT separate source observations and scoped assessments; a rating does not grant business truth |
| Regenerate every asset versus repair the implicated dependency | SELECT smallest responsible correction; broaden only when evidence shows the parent decision is affected |
| Attribute every CPA rise to creative versus inspect costs and response | SELECT decomposition and validity review before causal claims or replacement |
| Preserve only wins versus retain invalid/inconclusive/rejected work | SELECT complete permitted learning history to avoid repetition and survivorship bias |
| Transfer a winner unchanged versus check context and marginal effects | SELECT scoped reuse/scale decision with current constraints and authority |
| Build universal optimisation infrastructure versus domain-native records | SELECT readable contracts and external execution; infrastructure needs later justified evidence |

### Executable aggregate counterexamples

The following counts are expressly synthetic and independent of the text fixture. They demonstrate why an aggregate change cannot by itself identify its cause. No statistical claim or real fatigue diagnosis is made.

| Probe | Stipulated values | Calculated result and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| N01 composition shift | Early: H 64 clicks / 800 impressions; L 4 / 200. Later: H 16 / 200; L 16 / 800 | Overall CTR falls from 6.8% to 3.2%, while H stays at 8% and L at 2%. A common 50/50 mix is 5% in both periods; the supplied numbers do not establish within-placement deterioration |
| N02 cost change | Both periods: 10,000 impressions, 1,000 clicks, 50 qualified outcomes. Spend rises from GBP 1,000 to GBP 1,500 | CTR remains 10% and qualified outcomes per click 5%; cost per qualified outcome rises from GBP 20 to GBP 30. This arithmetic alone does not identify fatigue or the actual cause of cost change |

```python
from fractions import Fraction

# Synthetic aggregates only. No provider or campaign execution.
early = {"H": (64, 800), "L": (4, 200)}
later = {"H": (16, 200), "L": (16, 800)}

def rates(rows: dict[str, tuple[int, int]]) -> dict[str, Fraction]:
    out = {}
    for key, (clicks, impressions) in rows.items():
        if type(clicks) is not int or type(impressions) is not int:
            raise ValueError("Integer counts required for this illustration")
        if impressions <= 0 or clicks < 0 or clicks > impressions:
            raise ValueError("Invalid synthetic counts")
        out[key] = Fraction(clicks, impressions)
    return out

r0, r1 = rates(early), rates(later)
a0 = Fraction(sum(x for x, _ in early.values()), sum(n for _, n in early.values()))
a1 = Fraction(sum(x for x, _ in later.values()), sum(n for _, n in later.values()))
assert r0 == r1 == {"H": Fraction(2, 25), "L": Fraction(1, 50)}
assert a0 == Fraction(17, 250) and a1 == Fraction(4, 125)
assert a1 - a0 == Fraction(-9, 250)
assert sum(r0.values()) / 2 == sum(r1.values()) / 2 == Fraction(1, 20)
assert Fraction(1000, 10000) == Fraction(1, 10)
assert Fraction(50, 1000) == Fraction(1, 20)
assert Fraction(1000, 50) == 20 and Fraction(1500, 50) == 30
for bad in ({"X": (0, 0)}, {"X": (11, 10)}, {"X": (1.5, 10)}):
    try:
        rates(bad)
    except ValueError:
        pass
    else:
        raise AssertionError("Invalid synthetic counts accepted")
print("N01: aggregate 6.8% -> 3.2%; within-context rates unchanged; common mix 5%.")
print("N02: cost per qualified outcome GBP 20 -> GBP 30; response ratios unchanged.")
print("Synthetic arithmetic and invalid-input checks: PASS; no campaign executed.")
```

The count bounds above describe this synthetic clicks/impressions illustration, not a universal provider counting rule. The common-mix calculation is an arithmetic counterexample, not a fitted causal estimator or a recommendation to choose weights after seeing real outcomes. Real analysis retains Stage 8's design, units, uncertainty and predeclared decision rules.
