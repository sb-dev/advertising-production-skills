## 3. Candidate discovery and selection

The candidate pool was formed by crossing the five learning responsibilities with failure mechanisms in the accepted practice: commercial misunderstanding, unsupported proof, modality loss, uncontrolled variation, fragmented campaigns, invalid observations, wrong-layer repair and upstream economic harm. Format and platform variety were then checked against the coverage requirement; a platform name alone was not treated as an independently useful example.

Twenty-five candidates were compared, five per provisional level. These are the actual design alternatives considered, not an original minimum. ### Comparisons that determined the progression

| Alternative pair | Decision and trade-off |
|---|---|
| Five examples with larger appendices versus fifteen independent examples | Select fifteen: each teaches a distinct task with its own complete prompt and acceptance contract |
| Reuse pack showcases as primary examples versus a separate teaching set | Select separate cases: topical overlap is legitimate, but pack differential evidence and progressive production have different questions |
| All text-only demonstrations versus modality-appropriate outputs | Select real requested text, static, audio and video outputs; do not falsely call prompts finished media |
| All high-fidelity productions versus the cheapest useful representation | Match fidelity to the question: E01 needs exact copy, E03 needs audible output, E05 needs actual controlled video and E10 needs a trustworthy analysis |
| More formats at every level versus focused bounded work first | Keep L1 small, add controlled comparisons at L2 and integrated multi-format work at L3 |
| Fifteen unrelated headlines versus connected campaign reasoning | Add full source/claim/media/destination/test/repair relationships at higher levels; do not add complexity without a new decision |
| Hypothetical metrics presented as case studies versus explicit synthetic exercises | Keep all teaching data synthetic with complete units and limits; real product/source review remains distinct |
| Preferred concept versus proven campaign winner | Permit a reasoned preference for review; require actual valid evidence for performance conclusions |

## 5. Sources, synthetic data and authority

Each prompt embeds named SYN records with the facts needed for that exercise. An ID is a locator for supplied fixture text, not proof that a real document exists elsewhere. Product facts, prices and capacities do not migrate into a real consuming project without its actual approved inputs. The `.example` routes are intentionally not deployed; continuity can be designed and rendered offline while live readiness remains blocked.

All prompts prohibit inferred paid generation, external uploads, public hosting, account changes and media spend. This does not revoke any genuinely supplied scoped authority in a future real task; the executor must check actual authority and effects under Stage 12. Synthetic approvals do not grant real privileges. Original source/claim and Legal ownership survives pack selection.

E10–E15 provide deliberately bounded data rather than inviting invented analytics. E10 separates raw transport count, unique event and qualified eligibility. E11 provides disjoint impression contexts but not deduplicated people or a causal repetition experiment. E12 stipulates a mathematical refund boundary without presenting legal advice. E13 has stipulated random assignment within a fictional readout, not an actual study. E14/E15 are explicitly descriptive with absent causal assignment or full economics. No supplied platform total is substituted for verified business outcomes.

The example suite does not prove every pack's behavioural advantage. Stage 15's eight pack showcases, sixteen prompts and forty-eight later condition/case executions are preserved unchanged. Primary examples may use a pack to teach production; claiming pack maturity still requires the separate matched comparison evidence.
