## 3. Research method and evidence interpretation

A multi-pass public-source review covered the literal discipline list, then investigated gaps in channel constraints, production commitment, business-value measurement and operational repair. The [source register][Sources] identifies 35 accessed primary sources, their publisher, publication/update information, retrieval date, exact public material read and limitations. It includes professional associations, media/standards owners, platform documentation, original researchers' abstracts, advertising-code text and a historical adjudication.

The empirical or descriptive finding at the beginning of each topic is attributed to its source. The subsequent **Project application** and **Failure and repair** paragraphs are original recommendations for this repository, derived from the governing bootstrap and accepted ownership boundaries. They are not claims that a source prescribes our exact artefact names, that these recommendations have been experimentally validated, or that a real client has approved them.

Evidence strength is claim-specific. A platform manual can establish its documented configuration semantics; it cannot establish that a campaign is profitable. An agency role description establishes professional responsibilities, not a required multi-agent organisation chart. A public abstract supports its stated finding, not an unexamined statistical method. A historical ruling demonstrates a particular failure under its stated context; it does not clear a new advertisement. Vendor creative heuristics are candidate judgements, not mandatory universal formulas.

The review deliberately tests attractive generalisations against contrary constraints: adaptive delivery against controlled experimentation, attributed efficiency against causal lift, channel-native creativity against approved truth, and high-fidelity production against cheaper ways to resolve uncertainty. It also includes newer calibrated-prediction research so that scepticism about attribution does not become the unsupported claim that all non-experimental inference is useless. No live advertising or dataset replication was performed.

## 4. Discipline findings and project applications

### P01. Account planning

**Source finding.** APG connects planning to the client's business aims, a strategy and creative brief, collaboration with account/creative teams, and continuing effectiveness review. Account management coordinates the client relationship, deadlines and budgets. IPA's planner description adds research, creative-development judgement and lessons learned. [R01] [R02]

**Project application.** The planner responsibility should turn already approved business inputs into a question advertising can answer: what needs to change, for whom, in which context, with what evidence and limits? Inputs include the approved offer, audience evidence, commercial constraints and previous learning. Useful outputs are a campaign brief, evidence gaps, communication objective and decision rationale. A small project may have one person perform several roles, but approval authority must remain attributable rather than inferred from role labels.

A cheap first representation is a short brief with explicit unknowns and conflicting inputs, reviewed before creative production. Commitment occurs when the relevant owner accepts the campaign problem and boundaries, not when an attractive idea appears. Evidence review should ask whether each audience assertion is observed, inferred or still a hypothesis.

**Failure and repair.** A brief that merely restates “increase sales” gives creatives no bounded communication task. Repair the missing objective or evidence connection; do not invent a different customer segment, offer or budget. Conflicting commercial inputs go back to Business Building or the project owner. [Stage1]

### P02. Creative strategy

**Source finding.** IPA explicitly distinguishes the central advertising idea from its executional treatment and asks planners to assess ideas against insight and business need. Its art-direction guidance places concept development before approved final executions. [R02] [R04]

**Project application.** Creative strategy should make a reasoned choice about the message and why its expression might matter in the proposed audience and placement context. A territory is an avenue to explore, a concept is a communicable creative idea, and a treatment describes an execution. Calling three differently worded hooks three strategies would lose this distinction.

Inputs are the accepted brief, permissible claims, proof, brand constraints and channel context. Outputs should make alternatives comparable: intended response, core proposition, creative mechanism, evidence dependency, cheapest representation and reason to prefer or reject the route. Selection must identify what is approved to preserve and what remains provisional. Text descriptions, a rough frame sequence or a spoken sketch can answer conceptual questions before specialist production.

**Failure and repair.** A beautifully rendered concept may conceal a weak proposition or unsupported assumption. Return to the specific strategic premise instead of polishing the asset. Conversely, a weak crop does not invalidate an otherwise accepted concept. Rework only the treatment when the central idea still meets the brief. [Bootstrap] [Stage1]

### P03. Copywriting

**Source finding.** IPA treats copywriting as both idea development and execution across headlines, body copy, scripts and other media, working with art direction and overseeing production. CAP's substantiation guidance emphasises likely audience interpretation, not just the advertiser's intended meaning. [R03] [R32]

**Project application.** Copy direction should state the message hierarchy, proof permitted to support it, intended action, tone and material qualifications. A copywriter may draft directly in Advertising for bounded text work; specialist Narrative support is valuable when storytelling or script craft warrants it. The output is not merely fluent language: it must preserve the approved commercial exchange and support the intended next step.

A cheap review compares plain-text alternatives alongside their claim dependencies and intended placement. Read a script aloud for comprehension before recording; inspect short copy in its actual space rather than declaring it valid because it fits a character counter. Copy acceptance and legal/publication authority are separate decisions.

**Failure and repair.** Cutting “eligible customers” to improve a headline can change the claim. Restore or reformulate the affected assertion and qualification, then re-review its scope. Do not silently weaken truthful constraints to satisfy a platform's optimisation recommendation. A tonal mismatch can be repaired without changing the offer or all approved copy. [Stage1]

### P04. Art direction

**Source finding.** IPA identifies visual ideas, sketches, references, storyboards, commissioning and production oversight as art-direction work. It distinguishes the core idea from execution details that can change without destroying it. [R04]

**Project application.** The art-direction responsibility asks what the audience should notice and understand, not simply which visual style is fashionable. Inputs include the message/claim boundaries, concept, brand assets, intended format and usage constraints. Outputs can progress from composition notes and rough frames to a selected visual direction and a bounded specialist brief. References communicate intent; they do not prove ownership of an image, person, character or style asset.

Use inexpensive thumbnail sketches or a rough layout to test hierarchy before commissioning expensive craft. The commitment gate should identify the selected direction and which visual elements carry meaning. Production acceptance then examines the actual asset; an image prompt or storyboard is not a finished advertisement.

**Failure and repair.** Decorative complexity can obscure the brand, proof or CTA; visual treatment can also imply a product capability absent from the copy. Simplify the responsible layer or replace the misleading depiction. A layout fault should not trigger new claims, audience changes or wholesale regeneration of approved assets. Rights uncertainty returns to the appropriate specialist/owner. [Bootstrap] [Stage1]

### P05. Brand

**Source finding.** IPA's public effectiveness summary recommends integrating brand and activation and measuring both short- and long-term effects. LinkedIn's B2B Institute distinguishes reaching future buyers to build memory from capturing those already considering a purchase. [R06] [R17]

**Project application.** Brand work should be tied to what the campaign intends people to recognise, associate or remember, and how that supports the approved business objective. Treat identity consistency and communication effectiveness as related but separate: correct colours do not establish message recall, and immediate clicks do not fully describe a brand-building objective.

Inputs include existing brand decisions, audience/category evidence and the intended time horizon. Outputs should describe the desired association, recognisable elements to preserve and appropriate evidence. An inexpensive rough cross-format comparison can expose whether the same idea remains identifiable without funding all executions first. The brand owner approves departures from established identity or positioning.

**Failure and repair.** Reject both extremes: optimising all brand work for immediate conversion and accepting any expensive awareness activity as valuable because its effect is supposedly delayed. Ask for an explicit mechanism and a measurement plan suitable to the objective. Repair a lost recognisable element before replacing the campaign idea. Do not import a fixed brand/activation budget ratio as a universal rule. [Bootstrap]

### P06. Direct response

**Source finding.** Google's historical direct-response video case highlights an audience-related hook, pacing and a clear way to act. The case includes urgency tactics, but its results and historical product names are not current general instructions. CAP's current code separately constrains misleading claims, qualifications and pricing. [R07] [R31]

**Project application.** Direct response should connect an approved offer and supported reason to respond with an understandable, achievable action. The unit of review is the ad-to-destination promise, not a CTA considered alone. Inputs include price/terms, proof, likely objections, response route and downstream event meaning. Outputs are bounded copy/concept alternatives, the destination requirement and a hypothesis about qualified response.

A cheap check is to place the draft ad beside the proposed destination and ask what a reasonable reader expects to receive, pay and do. This is a semantic review, not a conversion-rate experiment. Offer approval precedes promotion; any new discount, guarantee or scarcity assertion requires its owner and evidence.

**Failure and repair.** Do not fabricate testimonials, deadlines or low-stock claims to strengthen urgency. If response is high but disappointed users abandon the next step, inspect promise continuity before rewriting the whole campaign. If a genuine commercial obstacle emerges, return it upstream rather than hiding it with more aggressive persuasion. [Stage1]

### P07. Performance marketing

**Source finding.** Google Ads documents that campaign goals and primary/secondary conversion-action configuration affect bidding and reporting. A secondary action included in a custom goal can still be used for bidding, so the label alone does not guarantee observation-only behaviour. [R08]

**Project application.** Performance work should state which outcome the delivery system is actually optimising and how that outcome relates to business value. Inputs include approved economics, event definitions, observation lag, delivery context and known measurement limitations. Outputs are an optimisation brief, diagnosis, bounded change recommendation and evidence required before further commitment. Ad-platform optimisation is an external executor, not a substitute for campaign judgement.

The inexpensive first check is a goal-to-event audit: would improving the configured event advance the approved objective, or merely make a dashboard look better? Verify the mapping before requesting more spend. Distinguish a business primary outcome from a platform's “primary” configuration field; their names do not make them semantically identical.

**Failure and repair.** Cheap sign-ups may be poor customers; abundant page views may be a mistakenly biddable proxy. Repair the objective mapping or event implementation through its owner and preserve the evidence of the previous configuration. Do not rewrite ads to solve a tracking failure or silently broaden the segment to reduce an apparent acquisition cost. [Stage1]

### P08. Search

**Source finding.** Responsive search ads can combine assets in different orders, and previews do not show every possible combination. Google documents placement controls for required text and warns that not every position is always shown. [R09]

**Project application.** Search work needs the approved offer, query-intent hypothesis, relevant message/proof and destination. Outputs should distinguish the intended audience from observed or proposed search contexts, and show how each permissible assembled message remains coherent. An asset set is not a conventional fixed headline-plus-body advertisement.

A cheap review should examine individually understandable assets and risky combinations, including the shortest serving configuration, qualifications and destination association. Preserve a required claim limitation with the claim, rather than assuming a preferred preview always serves. A later production model must record when automatic assembly or asset reuse changes the actual message being evaluated; this stage establishes the need, not the final schema.

**Failure and repair.** A missing qualification in one permitted combination requires repairing that asset/control relationship, not declaring the entire channel unusable. A query-to-destination mismatch requires intent or routing repair. Do not equate platform asset strength or attractive aggregate search performance with proof that the advertising created incremental business value. [Bootstrap] [Stage1]

### P09. Social

**Source finding.** TikTok's public guidance favours platform-native presentation, vertical framing, interface-safe space, deliberate sound and a hook/body/close structure. These are platform recommendations, not proof that all social advertisements should use one grammar. [R10]

**Project application.** Social creative should explain its attention mechanism without losing the supported message. Inputs include community/context evidence, approved brand and claims, placement behaviour, creator/asset rights and the experiment question. Outputs can include a rough opening, beat sequence, sound/text plan and a bounded variation matrix. “Creator-like” must not mean pretending an invented user is giving a genuine endorsement.

Cheap review should expose what the first frame communicates, what interface elements could cover and what remains understandable under the intended sound conditions. Select concepts before proliferating versions. Record which dimension changes, rather than calling unrelated hooks, offers and audiences one test.

**Failure and repair.** A trend can attract attention while communicating the wrong promise or dating rapidly. Repair the hook or contextual treatment when the approved idea survives. When falling response is suspected to be fatigue, preserve exposure context and other explanations; do not infer fatigue from elapsed time alone or replace every asset without diagnosis. [Bootstrap] [Stage1]

### P10. Video and CTV

**Source finding.** Clearcast separates script/substantiation review, a recommended but non-mandatory rough-cut stage, and final-file technical/compliance checks. IAB Tech Lab's CTV guide separates delivery, creative identification, measurement, fraud and suitability concerns; it is a use-case guide, not the underlying implementation specifications. [R11] [R12]

**Project application.** Video production should progress from accepted concept and script to a specialist-produced asset, maintaining the intended claim, proof, brand and response. CTV planning also needs the viewing environment and delivery partner's actual requirements; a conventional click-through route cannot simply be presumed. Outputs include a representation appropriate to the next decision, a bounded Video/Audio request, production evidence and a delivery specification owned by the executor.

The cheap gate is script, boards or a rough timing representation before final production. The expensive gate is acceptance of the actual finished file, with relevant clearance and delivery requirements checked separately. Creative approval does not authorise publication or spend.

**Failure and repair.** An unreadable disclosure, wrong edit length or missing identifying metadata is not repaired by changing the campaign objective. Request the specific asset or delivery correction. Do not treat a passed technical check as proof of legal clearance, campaign effectiveness or a correctly implemented CTV measurement stack. [Stage1]

### P11. Display and programmatic

**Source finding.** IAB Tech Lab's portfolio describes flexible aspect-ratio-based display units and LEAN principles for non-disruptive, lightweight experiences. Its CTV guide illustrates why inventory context, delivery, verification and suitability are distinct concerns in programmatic buying. [R13] [R12]

**Project application.** Display creative needs a hierarchy that survives the intended container, not just an exported size list. Programmatic is a buying/delivery mechanism, not a creative strategy. Inputs include approved concept and message, format/placement constraints, destination, brand-safety boundaries and an authorised media plan. Outputs include adaptable creative requirements and a precise external trafficking/verification request.

A cheap prototype should test the tightest useful layout and fallback meaning before a complete production family is commissioned. Confirm that a chosen publisher/product supports the intended format; a standards overview does not prove adoption by every supplier. Contextual suitability and technical renderability require separate evidence.

**Failure and repair.** A clipped CTA, slow-loading asset, wrong placement or inappropriate context should be diagnosed at its own layer. Repair composition, technical asset or media execution as appropriate. Do not build a DSP, auction protocol, ad server or universal buying abstraction inside Advertising merely because its handoff needs to name those external systems. [Stage1]

### P12. Out-of-home advertising

**Source finding.** JCDecaux's public portfolio table permits different levels of motion for different products; digital does not universally mean full-motion video. OAAA describes environmental mockups with distance views. JCDecaux also publishes landlord/partner acceptance restrictions beyond generic creative requirements. [R14] [R15] [R34]

**Project application.** OOH work should make the viewing situation explicit: site type, distance, likely dwell, orientation, surrounding context and permitted motion. Inputs include the approved concept/message, chosen inventory and media-owner requirements. Outputs include a site-aware creative brief, relevant static/motion treatment and a delivery/approval request. Location context can be part of the idea rather than a distribution detail.

A cheap representation places a rough composition in an environmental view and reviews what can be understood under the proposed viewing conditions. This record researches that practice; it does not claim an OAAA tool run or a validated legibility test. Exact site requirements and acceptance still need checking before production commitment.

**Failure and repair.** A mobile-social video may fail because the purchased screen permits only static work, or because its message depends on attention unavailable at the site. Repair the treatment or placement choice within approved authority. Do not assume a QR code or motion effect is allowed everywhere, or remove essential qualifications to meet a crude word-count maxim. [Stage1]

### P13. Audio

**Source finding.** Spotify's guidance recommends a focused message, conversational pacing, an audible brand and CTA, and sound design that does not compete with speech. Its public recommendations include study-derived performance claims, which are not adopted as guarantees. [R16]

**Project application.** Audio direction should specify what the listener must understand without relying on a visible screen. Inputs include approved script/message, brand/voice constraints, duration/context, pronunciation, response route, rights and required qualifications. Outputs include a timed script or rough read, sound direction and a specialist production request, followed by evaluation of the actual audio.

A cheap read-through can expose an overloaded script before voice or music production. Silence, pacing and intelligibility are deliberate design variables, not unused capacity to fill with more claims. The final gate must concern the delivered sound, because readable text does not establish audible qualification or a usable spoken URL.

**Failure and repair.** If music obscures the offer or the CTA is hard to understand, repair the mix or wording through the appropriate owner; do not assume louder or faster is better. A companion visual cannot silently carry information the intended screenless listener must receive. Rights or voice-authority uncertainty is a separate blocker, not an audio-quality score. [Stage1]

### P14. B2B demand generation

**Source finding.** LinkedIn's B2B Institute separates memory-building for future buyers from immediate lead capture. Its enterprise buying-group research with Bain describes product evaluators and less digitally visible risk-focused participants such as procurement, legal and finance. [R17] [R18]

**Project application.** A B2B brief should identify the buying situation and what the advertising is intended to advance: recognition, confidence, qualified conversation or another approved outcome. An individual form submission is not automatically a buying group's agreement or a sales-qualified opportunity. Inputs include approved segment/offer, evidence about buying roles, proof, sales-cycle context and a definition of useful response supplied by the business owner.

Outputs should distinguish message needs and proof for the relevant decision, with a destination capable of honouring the promise. A cheap review can compare product-value and vendor-risk questions against the available evidence. It must not invent references, client logos, compliance status or procurement acceptance to make a small supplier look established.

**Failure and repair.** High lead volume with poor fit may indicate a qualification, promise or audience problem. Diagnose that layer before increasing spend. Missing procurement proof returns to its owner; the ad cannot manufacture it. Do not impose an enterprise committee or fixed “95–5” split on the one-person consultancy stress test without evidence that it applies. [Stage1]

### P15. App acquisition

**Source finding.** Google's current API documentation distinguishes install, in-app action, value, engagement and pre-registration goals. Apple's AdAttributionKit overview describes signed attribution postbacks with privacy-limited information and crowd-anonymity-dependent detail. [R19] [R20]

**Project application.** App advertising should say whether success means an install, first useful action, paid subscription or retained value; these cannot be substituted merely because one is easier to observe. Inputs include the approved offer, supported markets, actual store/app availability, event meaning, attribution limitations and destination/deep-link requirements. Outputs should connect the creative promise to the store listing and post-install experience, with a measurement handoff to Software Engineering.

The cheap check is a promise-and-event walkthrough from ad to store to app. Establish whether the required observation is technically and legitimately available before choosing an optimisation event or measurement claim. Campaign readiness must not imply instrumentation has been implemented just because documentation describes it.

**Failure and repair.** Cheap installs with no useful behaviour require checking the promise, onboarding, event semantics and audience evidence. Delayed or privacy-limited postbacks are not permission to invent user-level histories. An unavailable market or broken deep link is repaired by the appropriate destination/execution owner, not by generating a more persuasive advertisement. [Stage1]

### P16. Retargeting

**Source finding.** Google documents re-engagement using prior website, app and other interactions. It also states that under optimised targeting, chosen audience segments can act as signals and ads may reach people outside those selections. [R21]

**Project application.** Retargeting should explain why the previous interaction makes a particular message relevant, while preserving data-use authority and the approved customer boundary. Inputs include the legitimate source and meaning of membership, recency/eligibility requirements, exclusions, already-completed actions and permitted delivery settings. Outputs are an audience hypothesis, message purpose, control requirements and a measurement caveat.

A cheap eligibility review should test synthetic boundary cases such as a recent purchaser, stale visitor or excluded individual without copying personal data into this repository. Verify whether the proposed executor can enforce hard restrictions; a useful optimisation signal is not an enforceable exclusion or consent record.

**Failure and repair.** Showing acquisition copy to completed customers can be an audience-state or suppression failure. Repair that mapping before changing every creative. Strong attributed conversion among already interested visitors does not, by itself, establish incremental effect; use the evidence appropriate to the decision. Do not solve enforcement limitations by quietly accepting delivery beyond an approved restriction. [Bootstrap] [Stage1]

### P17. Media planning

**Source finding.** IPA describes research-led channel choices aligned with communications strategy, coordination with creative teams and media buyers, feasibility checks and reporting against time, budget and effectiveness. [R22]

**Project application.** Media planning should state the job of each channel and placement, the audience/context hypothesis, scheduling and frequency considerations, budget proposal, exclusions and evidence needed to judge its contribution. The approved business-wide acquisition approach remains upstream; a media planner can propose but not silently authorise a new commercial channel strategy.

Useful artefacts are a channel-role rationale, placement/format requirements, staged allocation proposal and execution brief. A cheap feasibility pass should ask whether the concept works in the proposed inventory, whether sufficient delivery and measurement are possible, and whether the business can fulfil the response. Forecasts must be labelled estimates with their inputs, not campaign results.

**Failure and repair.** A creative that requires long attention cannot be rescued merely by buying cheap impressions in an unsuitable context. Repair the placement or treatment relationship. A budget recommendation requires separate spend approval; credentials and an approved creative are not permission. Where reach or frequency cannot be credibly deduplicated across systems, retain the uncertainty rather than adding incomparable figures. [Stage1]

### P18. Ad operations

**Source finding.** Campaign Manager 360's tracking-ad procedure distinguishes ads, creatives, placements, assignments, rotation and scheduling. It describes different behaviour for static and dynamic landing URLs and explicitly requires partners to validate implementation before launch because errors can distort counts. [R23]

**Project application.** Ad operations turns an authorised plan into correct external execution. Advertising should provide the intended asset/version, destination, placement, audience constraints, schedule, measurement requirements and approval scope; the executor returns identifiable configuration and delivery evidence. This is not a reason for Advertising to own credentials, account administration or an ad-serving implementation.

The cheap gate is a trafficking manifest and test-environment check before activating spend. Review what would actually run, not just the planning document. Keep configuration revisions attributable so that later diagnosis can distinguish a creative hypothesis from the way it was delivered.

**Failure and repair.** Missing assignments, inactive status, stale exported URLs, inconsistent schedules or incorrectly implemented tags can prevent delivery or corrupt evidence. Request the smallest authorised configuration/tag repair and verify it. A reporting discrepancy does not establish poor creative performance; preserve the affected observation period as unreliable until its limitation is understood. [Stage1]

### P19. Testing

**Source finding.** Google's custom-experiment procedure shares traffic and budget with a comparison campaign, distinguishes assignment methods and warns that in-flight changes complicate interpretation. Not every campaign type supports the same experiment mechanism. [R24]

**Project application.** A test must begin with a question and identifiable contrast. Inputs include the decision to be informed, hypothesis, controlled change, comparison, relevant outcomes, guardrails, measurement feasibility and spend authority. Outputs are a test brief and, only after actual execution, an evidence record with limitations and a bounded decision. This stage does not choose a universal test duration, sample size or significance threshold.

Cheap concept review and a live performance experiment answer different questions. Use review to remove incoherence or unsupported claims before spending; do not call a review panel, AI opinion or platform asset ranking causal evidence. Before commitment, identify the randomisation/comparison unit, interference risks, observation window and changes that would invalidate interpretation.

**Failure and repair.** Changing offer, audience and creative simultaneously prevents a narrow creative attribution unless that combined treatment was the intended hypothesis. Repair the comparison design, or report the broader treatment honestly. Sparse or inconclusive data should not be converted into a declared winner merely because a delivery system allocated more traffic to one variant. [Bootstrap] [Stage1]

### P20. Attribution

**Source finding.** Google describes DDA as account-specific allocation of credit across observed conversion paths. Original Facebook experiment research found that the examined observational methods often differed materially from randomised estimates; later work repeated the concern at greater scale. A 2026 working paper proposes experiment-trained prediction for other campaigns. Google also describes user- or geography-based Conversion Lift studies, with access and volume limitations. These are different evidence-generating approaches, not interchangeable labels. [R25] [R26] [R27] [R28] [R29]

**Project application.** Measurement reporting should identify the observation source, event definition, window, allocation method, aggregation and known limitations before comparing numbers. Attribution answers a credit-assignment question under a model; a causal claim needs the design and assumptions supporting it. Experiment-calibrated predictions should remain labelled predictions rather than direct measurements of an untested campaign.

A cheap reconciliation can expose mismatched event definitions, time windows or platforms claiming the same business outcome before commissioning a new model. Software/analytics specialists own collection and computation. Advertising owns the decision question and interpretation within the available evidence.

**Failure and repair.** Do not sum platform-attributed conversions into unique acquired customers without valid reconciliation. Do not claim a change in attribution settings created revenue. Repair the evidence mapping or revise the strength of the conclusion. Neither “all attribution is truth” nor “all modelling is useless” follows from the reviewed sources. [Bootstrap] [Stage1]

### P21. Brand lift

**Source finding.** Google Brand Lift compares survey responses from exposed and eligible-but-unexposed groups on brand outcomes such as recall, awareness and consideration. It reports different forms of lift and response counts, and access is not available to every account. [R30]

**Project application.** A brand-lift question should state which perception the campaign intends to change and why it matters to the approved objective. Inputs include the brand/product definition, proposed survey construct, comparison design, eligible population and feasibility. Outputs are a study requirement and, after actual execution, results whose unit, baseline and uncertainty remain visible.

The cheap preflight is to inspect whether the proposed question measures the intended construct and whether a supported study can actually be run. Avoid promising a platform study before access, budget and response-volume needs are established. A survey plan is not a measured attitude change, and a positive attitude change is not automatically an observed financial return.

**Failure and repair.** A result can be uninformative because of insufficient responses, the wrong brand grouping, an ambiguous construct or a compromised comparison. Repair the study or qualify the conclusion rather than inventing lift. A failed immediate-sales metric does not alone falsify a brand objective, but the objective still needs accountable evidence. [Bootstrap] [Stage1]

### P22. Campaign QA

**Source finding.** Clearcast reviews actual final-file technical and compliance properties, while Google's conversion diagnostic procedure requires a test conversion and has stated prerequisites and limitations. A historical ASA paid-search ruling demonstrates that additional destination information did not resolve material qualifications missing from that specific ad. [R11] [R35] [R33]

**Project application.** QA should separate strategic coherence, truth/proof, brand/craft, placement fit, destination continuity, delivery configuration and measurement readiness. Inputs are the approved decisions and exact proposed artefacts/configuration, not an unversioned folder of plausible assets. Outputs are attributable findings, severity, affected scope, repair owner and verification evidence. A single “quality score” cannot replace these independent decisions.

Cheap preflight uses the smallest representation capable of exposing each risk: copy for a claim, a contextual layout for hierarchy, a timed rough for pacing, and a test event for instrumentation. Final release acceptance must inspect actual assets and the intended external setup. Each change reopens the relevant checks without invalidating unaffected approved work unnecessarily.

**Failure and repair.** A campaign can be persuasive but unsupported, technically deliverable but misleading, or correctly served but unmeasurable. Block or repair the failed layer; do not average it away against good creative ratings. QA completion is not autonomous legal clearance, spend authority or proof of commercial effectiveness. [Bootstrap] [Stage1]

## 5. Production progression, artefacts and commitment

The following is an original project synthesis of the researched practice, constrained by the bootstrap. It is a responsibility/decision map, not a new runtime, fixed agency staffing model or final lifecycle schema. Cheap representations are chosen for the uncertainty they can resolve, not because every campaign must produce every document. The ISBA/IPA/APA production-pitch summary separately identifies treatments, production specifications, budgets and award decisions; this supports keeping the production commitment explicit rather than treating a concept selection as unlimited authorisation. [R05]

| Decision point | Useful inputs and artefacts | Cheapest responsible evidence | Commitment and owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is the problem ready for advertising work? | Approved commercial inputs; evidence gaps; bounded brief | Resolve contradictions and explain the intended communication change | Business-input owner retains commercial authority; Advertising accepts its scoped task |
| Which strategic route is worth developing? | Message/proof boundaries; alternative concepts and rationale | Text, rough frames or another representation that exposes the idea | Appropriate creative/brand owner selects a route without authorising final production or spend |
| Is production sufficiently specified? | Selected concept; specialist brief; fidelity, rights and delivery constraints | Treatment, rough timing/layout and unresolved-risk review | Production commitment is bounded; unapproved claim/offer changes return to their owner |
| Does the execution preserve the decision? | Actual delivered asset and provenance; approved reference decisions | Review at the intended viewing/listening conditions | Craft acceptance and advertising integration evaluation are separate from legal/publication authority |
| Is the proposed launch responsible? | Media/test plan; destination continuity; exact external setup; applicable approvals | Preflight, test-event and configuration evidence from the authorised executor | Publication and spend require explicit, scoped authorisation |
| What does the evidence justify next? | Delivery/context, platform and downstream observations, limitations | Diagnose the failure layer before commissioning replacement work | Advertising recommends a bounded change; upstream or specialist owners act within their remit |

A production gate is meaningful only if it changes the permissible next commitment. A document marked “approved” without an identifiable authority, artefact and scope is not adequate. Research-informed recommendations do not supply the missing authority. Neither API access nor a vendor's suggested budget creates permission to spend. [Stage1]

### Role composition without bureaucracy

The reusable capability needs planning judgement, creative strategy, words, visual/audio craft direction, media judgement, measurement interpretation and QA. It does not require a separate agent, meeting or department for each. One operator can fulfil multiple responsibilities if inputs, outputs, conflicts and authority remain explicit. Specialist delegation should be triggered by actual craft, legal or technical needs, not by simulating an agency organisation chart. [Bootstrap] [Stage1]

Account coordination preserves the brief and decision trail. Planning tests whether a communication choice answers the problem. Creative responsibilities express it. Media connects expression to context. Operations implements authorised delivery. Measurement judges what can be learned. QA checks that none of these silently bypasses another. This division is a project synthesis of P01–P22, not a claim that every professional team uses identical job titles.

## 6. Approach comparisons and rejected generalisations

| Comparison | Useful distinction | Trade-off and decision for this project |
|---|---|---|
| Brand building versus direct response | Longer-horizon memory/association versus an immediate response objective | Preserve the intended effect and its measurement horizon. Either can be relevant; neither justifies unsupported claims or a universal budget ratio. P05–P07 |
| Strategic concept versus execution variant | Different creative mechanisms versus altered expressions of one selected idea | Compare ideas before expensive production; do not count cosmetic changes as conceptual diversity or replace the concept for a local production defect. P02–P04 |
| High-fidelity preview versus decision-useful rough | A polished asset can answer craft questions; a rough may answer the strategic question more cheaply | Choose fidelity by unresolved risk. Do not mistake a rough for a finished asset or a render for evidence of effectiveness. P04, P10, P13 |
| One master resized versus context-specific adaptation | Shared message/identity versus different reading, listening, interaction and placement conditions | Preserve meaning while adapting expression. Constraints are not merely dimensions: search assembly, OOH motion and audio comprehension demand different checks. P08–P13 |
| Narrow audience hypothesis versus automated expansion | A campaign reasoning boundary versus a delivery system's optimisation signal | Verify enforceability before claiming restrictions hold. A platform limitation cannot waive a business, safety or legal constraint. P07, P16 |
| Platform efficiency versus downstream value | Configured event/cost performance versus qualified response and business contribution | Keep event semantics and provenance visible; route economics or capacity problems upstream. P07, P14, P15 |
| Adaptive delivery versus controlled test | Allocation to a system's current estimate versus a designed comparison answering a hypothesis | Useful optimisation evidence is not automatically causal experiment evidence. Preserve assignment, changes, confounders and inconclusive outcomes. P19 |
| Attribution versus incrementality | Model-based credit allocation versus a counterfactual effect question | Use each for its appropriate decision. Document assumptions, windows and reconciliation; avoid both blind attribution and blanket rejection of models. P20 |
| Direct experiment versus calibrated prediction | Evidence for a tested intervention versus inference transferred from experimental training evidence | Newer methods may be useful, but require validation and an explicit prediction label. This stage selects no measurement engine. P20 |
| Legal/claim review versus technical acceptance | Whether the communication is supportable/permitted versus whether an asset/configuration operates | Independent failures need independent repairs. No averaging, inferred clearance or silent promotion from one approval to another. P10, P18, P22 |
| Reusable advertising expertise versus universal marketing platform | Domain judgement and handoffs versus accounts, CRM, rendering, tracking, serving and lifecycle infrastructure | Keep external systems external and Pactwright optional. Reuse no universal abstraction merely because several practices have documents named “brief”. [Stage1] |

Rejected alternatives include a prompt-only copy factory, platform recommendations treated as business strategy, a universal cross-channel quality score, autonomous budget changes, mandatory high-fidelity generation before concept selection, and simulated consumer/approver responses presented as real evidence. Each conflicts with the original bootstrap or the distinctions established above.

### Uncertainty and limitations carried forward

This is not a full literature review, campaign casebook, all-platform policy audit or software capability test. The legal evidence is deliberately scoped; current execution requires the Stage 10 review and campaign-specific facts. Exact platform behaviour may change after retrieval or differ by account, region, objective and product. Dynamic guidance with stale statements is evidence to verify, not a reason to infer availability.

Public abstracts establish the described methodological concern and research direction, not detailed replication. No source supplies actual audience truth, economics, approvals, test outcomes or production capability for Kakeibo, the consultancy or the Production Skills ecosystem. Those must be obtained or explicitly constructed as synthetic inputs in their proper stages, without promoting them to real-world evidence.

No mandatory Stage 2 question remains unresolved. Optional inaccessible reports were not used as if read. There is no missing user decision needed to finish generic professional-practice research, and no waiver of subsequent-stage requirements.
